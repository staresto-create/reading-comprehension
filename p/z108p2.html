The Science and Application of Hypnosis

文章

第 1 段When Dr. David Spiegel emerged from a three-hour shoulder surgery in 1972, he didn’t use any pain medication to recover. Instead, he hypnotized himself. It worked—to the surprise of everyone but Spiegel himself, who has studied hypnosis for 45 years.

第 2 段Hypnosis is often misunderstood as a sleep-like state in which a person is put to sleep and does whatever he is asked to do. But according to Dr. Spiegel, it is a state of highly focused attention and intense concentration. Being hypnotized, you tune out most of the stimuli around you. You focus intently on the subject at hand, to the near exclusion of any other thought. This trance-like state can be an effective tool to control pain, ease anxiety, and deal with stress.

第 3 段Not all people, however, are equally hypnotizable. In a recent study, Dr. Spiegel and his colleagues found that people who are easily hypnotized tend to be more trusting of others, more intuitive, and more likely to get caught up in a good movie. The research team compared people who were highly hypnotizable with those low in hypnotizability. Both groups were given fMRI scans during several different conditions: at rest, while recalling a memory, and during two sessions of hypnotism. The researchers saw some interesting changes in the brain during hypnosis—but only in the highly hypnotizable group. Specifically, there was a drop in activity in the part of the brain which usually fires up when there is something to worry about.

第 4 段This helps explain how hypnosis can have powerful effects, including reducing stress, anxiety, pain, and self-consciousness. Spiegel hopes that the practice can be used to replace painkillers. His own previous research has shown that when people in pain were taught self-hypnosis, they needed half the pain medication and suffered half the pain of those who were only given access to painkillers. However, more needs to be learned about hypnosis in order to harness its potential effects.

第 43 題 推論題 Inference

第 3 段more likely to get caught up in a good movie.

原文 → 正確選項
caught up in a good movie → characters in fictions
第 3 段說容易催眠的人，看到一部好電影，比較容易沉浸其中（get caught up in a good movie）。看電影容易入戲，就是容易把自己投入片中角色的處境，電影也是虛構的故事，選項換成 identify themselves with characters in fictions，所以是 (C)。最容易選錯的是 (D)，這個選項照抄 trust：原文說的是容易催眠的人比較信任別人（more trusting of others），trusting 是「信任別人」，選項的 trustworthy 是「值得別人信任」，兩個字意思不同。(B) 照抄第 2 段的 sleep，把催眠當成睡著，正是 Spiegel 說的誤解；(A) 容易催眠的人比較信任別人，看不出他們和社會隔絕。

逐段中譯

第 1 段1972 年，David Spiegel 醫師動完三個小時的肩部手術，復原期間沒有用任何止痛藥，而是替自己催眠。結果真的有效。除了研究催眠已經 45 年的 Spiegel 本人，大家都很驚訝。

第 2 段很多人誤以為催眠是一種像睡覺一樣的狀態，人在這種狀態下睡著了，別人叫他做什麼，他就做什麼。不過，根據 Spiegel 醫師的說法，催眠是一種高度專注、注意力非常集中的狀態。處在催眠狀態時，你會把周遭大部分的刺激都屏除在外，全神貫注在眼前的事情上，幾乎不去想任何別的事情。這種恍惚般的狀態，可以是控制疼痛、緩解焦慮、應付壓力的有效工具。

第 3 段不過，並不是每個人都一樣容易催眠。Spiegel 醫師和同事在最近一項研究中發現，容易催眠的人往往比較信任別人，比較依賴直覺，看到一部好電影，也比較容易沉浸其中。研究團隊把很容易催眠的人和不容易催眠的人做比較，兩組人都在幾種不同的情況下接受功能性磁振造影（fMRI）掃描：休息時、回想一段記憶時，以及兩次催眠過程中。研究人員看到大腦在催眠時出現一些有趣的變化，但只出現在容易催眠的那一組。具體來說，大腦中通常在有事情要擔心時就會活躍起來的區域，活動下降了。

第 4 段這有助於解釋催眠為什麼能有強大的效果，包括減輕壓力、焦慮、疼痛和自我意識。Spiegel 希望催眠可以用來取代止痛藥。他自己先前的研究顯示，身體疼痛的人學了自我催眠以後，和只用止痛藥的人相比，需要的止痛藥只有一半，感受到的疼痛也只有一半。不過，要善用催眠的潛在效果，對催眠還需要更多的了解。

字詞搭配 配對與接法

「字詞搭配」是要整組記的說法，分兩種：一種是哪兩個字配在一起，例如 pose a serious risk（造成嚴重的風險）、in unison（齊聲），換成意思相近的字就不自然；另一種是這個字後面要接什麼，寫成公式，例如 be resistant to + N（對……有抵抗力）。公式裡的 + N 表示接名詞，+ V 接原形動詞，+ V-ing 接動名詞，sb 指人，sth 指事物，「時間」「地方」這類中文表示那裡要填的東西。名稱不必背，看得出這一條寫的是「配對」還是「接法」就夠了；記住搭配和接法，這個字你才用得出來，不會只是看得懂。一看就懂的組合和專有名詞不列。每一條寫的是查字典時的基本形，文中的寫法不同時，下面附上原文。
  • emerge from + N從……出來、結束……第 1 段原文：emerged from a three-hour shoulder surgery
  • to the surprise of + sb讓某人意外的是第 1 段原文：to the surprise of everyone but Spiegel himself
  • misunderstand A as B把 A 誤解成 B第 2 段原文：is often misunderstood as a sleep-like state
  • put + sb + to sleep讓某人睡著第 2 段原文：a person is put to sleep
  • tune out + N不去理會……第 2 段原文：you tune out most of the stimuli around you
  • focus on + N專注在……第 2 段原文：You focus intently on the subject at hand
  • at hand眼前的第 2 段原文：the subject at hand
  • to the exclusion of + N完全排除……第 2 段原文：to the near exclusion of any other thought
  • deal with stress應付壓力第 2 段
  • be trusting of + sb容易相信某人第 3 段原文：be more trusting of others
  • get caught up in + N沉浸在……之中第 3 段原文：get caught up in a good movie
  • at rest休息時第 3 段
  • a drop in + N……下降第 3 段原文：there was a drop in activity
  • have access to + N可以取得……第 4 段原文：were only given access to painkillers
  • harness + N善用……第 4 段原文：harness its potential effects
