The Nature and Tasks of Philosophy
學測 95課綱（98年施測）第 53–56 題4 題

文章

Philosophy is not a subject but an activity. You don’t study it; you do it. To be a philosopher, therefore, you need not be an academic, or an expert on the history of the practice of philosophy. However, you do need to think and argue in a particular way. You might already be a philosopher without knowing it.

The word “philosophy,” which means “love of knowledge,” comes from the Greek language. The earliest philosophers among the ancient Greeks were also mathematicians and scientists. These were people who had the tendency and also, crucially, the time to seek knowledge, because they had slaves to do all of the labor work.

Broadly speaking, there are two tasks for philosophers. The first is to search for truth. However, many philosophers say they don’t know what the word “truth” means, while other philosophers don’t think it exists at all. The search for truth is not the central concern of most academic philosophers, but that doesn’t mean everyone else shouldn’t have a go at it.

The second task is about the application of logical thinking to everyday questions, concepts, and ideas that are often taken for granted. This kind of conceptual analysis serves to clarify debate over the meaning of such concepts as justice, knowledge, and human nature. There is no point arguing about justice, knowledge, perception, or human nature unless we have some idea about what kind of things these terms might mean.

綱要
  1. (therefore) 定義哲學為一種活動而非學科，任何人無需學術背景都可成為哲學家，只要 (However) 以特定方式思考和論證。
  2. 解釋「哲學」一詞源自希臘語，意為「愛知識」，古代希臘哲學家同時也是數學家和科學家，他們有時間追求知識。
  3. (Broadly speaking) 闡述哲學家的第一個任務：探索真理，但真理的定義與存在性有爭議。
  4. (The second task is about) 闡述哲學家的第二個任務：將邏輯思維應用於日常概念分析，(unless) 以澄清諸如正義、知識、人性等詞語的意義。
