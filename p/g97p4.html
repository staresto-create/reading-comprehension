97 學測閱讀測驗：疼痛的心理與生理元素

Physical and Psychological Elements of Pain
學測 97年第 53–56 題4 題

文章

第 1 段If you touch your finger to a hot stove, you know it’s going to hurt. However, if you convince yourself beforehand that the pain won’t be so bad, you might not suffer as much. According to a recent study, the part of your brain that reacts to severe pain is largely the same part that reacts to expectation of pain.

第 2 段Researchers in this study worked with 10 volunteers, ages 24 to 46. Each volunteer wore a device that gave out 20-second-long pulses of heat to the right leg. There were three levels of heat, producing mild, moderate, or strong pain. During training, the volunteers would first hear a tone, followed by a period of silence, and then feel a heat pulse. They then learned to associate the length of the silent pause with the intensity of the upcoming heat pulse. The longer the pause, the stronger the heat pulse would be, causing more severe pain.

第 3 段A day or two later, the real experiment began. The researchers found that the parts of the brain involved in learning, memory, emotion, and touch became more active as the volunteers expected higher levels of pain. These were mainly the same areas that became active when participants actually felt pain. Interestingly, when the volunteers expected only mild or moderate pain but experienced severe pain, they reported feeling 28 percent less pain than when they expected severe pain and actually got it.

第 4 段The new study emphasizes that pain has both physical and psychological elements. Understanding how pain works in the mind and brain could eventually give doctors tools for helping people cope with painful medical treatments.

第 56 題 推論題 Inference

第 4 段Understanding how pain works in the mind and brain could eventually give doctors tools for helping people cope with painful medical treatments.

逐段中譯

第 1 段如果你用手指碰到熱爐子，你知道會痛。然而，如果你事先說服自己不會那麼痛，你可能就不會那麼難受。根據最近的一項研究，大腦裡對劇痛起反應的部位，和對預期疼痛起反應的部位，大致是同一個。

第 2 段這項研究的研究人員找了 10 位志願者，年齡從 24 到 46 歲。每位志願者都戴著一個裝置，裝置會對右腿發出每次 20 秒的熱脈衝。熱度分三個等級，分別造成輕微、中度或強烈的疼痛。訓練期間，志願者會先聽到一個聲音，接著是一段靜默，然後感覺到熱脈衝。他們因此學會把靜默停頓的長短，和接下來熱脈衝的強度聯想在一起：停頓越長，熱脈衝就越強，造成的疼痛也越劇烈。

第 3 段一兩天後，正式的實驗開始了。研究人員發現，志願者預期的疼痛越強，大腦裡和學習、記憶、情緒、觸覺有關的部位就越活躍。這些部位和參與者真的感覺到疼痛時活躍的部位，大致相同。有意思的是，志願者如果預期的只是輕微或中度的疼痛，實際上卻感受到劇痛，他們說感覺到的疼痛，比預期劇痛而真的遇到劇痛時少了 28%。

第 4 段這項新研究強調，疼痛同時有生理和心理的成分。了解疼痛在心智和大腦中怎麼運作，最後可能讓醫生有工具，幫助病人面對會痛的醫療處置。
