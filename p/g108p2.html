108 學測閱讀測驗：北美原住民的圖騰柱

Totem Poles in North America
學測 108年第 45–48 題4 題

文章

第 1 段Totem poles in North America are poles or posts carved with symbols or figures by Aboriginal peoples of the Northwest Coast. Carved from large, straight trees and painted vibrant colors, the totem poles are not just unique works of art. The coastal peoples have long passed on knowledge from generation to generation through oral traditions, and totem poles were the traditional way of telling the story of an individual family or clan.

第 2 段The totem pole can be grouped into specific categories, depending on its location and the occasion for which it was carved. Welcome poles were traditionally placed on village beachfronts to greet visitors arriving by canoe. Inside the homes of high-ranking chiefs is where house poles were found. The family’s history was carefully carved into each pole. Placed along the rear or front walls of a house, house poles also helped to support the main beam of the roof.

第 3 段Memorial poles stood in front of a house. They were erected in memory of a deceased chief or a high-ranking clan member. The poles depicted the person’s accomplishments or family history. Mortuary poles were also raised to honor the dead, but they differed from memorial poles, having a burial box placed at the top of the pole. Inside the burial box were the remains of the deceased.

第 4 段While many of these poles can still be found in various locations on the west coast of North America, there is one pole that can now only be found in a museum—the shame pole. Traditionally, shame poles were carved for a chief to embarrass and ridicule another who had done something wrong. Once the wrong was made right, the pole was taken down.

第 5 段Totem poles are important expressions of specific Aboriginal cultures. Despite the threats posed by cultural and political encroachment of colonial forces, the art of totem pole carving has survived. Aboriginal carvers continue to carve totems as symbols of their cultural pride and clan kinship.

逐段中譯

第 1 段北美的圖騰柱，是西北海岸的原住民刻上符號或人物圖案的柱子。圖騰柱取材自高大筆直的樹木，雕刻之後漆上鮮豔的色彩，不只是獨特的藝術品。沿海的原住民長久以來靠口述傳統，把知識一代一代傳下去，圖騰柱就是他們講述某個家族或氏族故事的傳統方式。

第 2 段圖騰柱可以依照設立的位置和雕刻的場合，分成幾個類別。迎賓柱傳統上立在村落的海灘邊，迎接划獨木舟來訪的客人。屋柱則設在地位崇高的酋長家中，每一根都仔細刻上家族的歷史。屋柱立在房屋的後牆或前牆邊，也幫忙支撐屋頂的大梁。

第 3 段紀念柱立在房屋前面，用來紀念過世的酋長或地位崇高的氏族成員，柱上刻畫這個人的功績或家族歷史。墓葬柱同樣是為了紀念死者而立，但和紀念柱不同的是，墓葬柱的頂端放了一個葬箱，裡面安放著死者的遺骸。

第 4 段這些圖騰柱有許多到現在仍然可以在北美西岸各地看到，但有一種柱子現在只能在博物館裡看到，那就是羞辱柱。傳統上，酋長會刻羞辱柱，讓另一位做錯事的酋長難堪、受人嘲笑；等對方把錯誤彌補好，柱子就會拆下來。

第 5 段圖騰柱是特定原住民文化的重要表現。儘管殖民勢力在文化和政治上的侵逼帶來威脅，圖騰柱的雕刻藝術仍然流傳了下來。原住民雕刻師至今仍持續雕刻圖騰柱，作為他們文化自豪與氏族情誼的象徵。
