110 學測閱讀測驗：仙人掌的再生能源應用

Prickly Pear Cactus as Renewable Energy
學測 110年第 41–44 題4 題

文章

第 1 段The prickly pear cactus is such a powerful symbol in Mexico that it occupies a prime spot on Mexico’s national flag. The plant was considered sacred by the ancient Aztecs, and modern-day Mexicans eat it, drink it, and even use it in medicines and shampoos. Now scientists have come up with a new use for the bright green plant: producing renewable energy.

第 2 段Known locally as nopal, the prickly pear is farmed on a massive scale in Mexico. Over the years, only the edible soft inner flesh has been used. The cactus’s thick outer layer (i.e., husk), with all those spines, had always been viewed as a waste product until researchers developed a biogas generator to turn the husks into electricity.

第 3 段The pilot project dedicated to developing the cactus biogas generator began in the south of Mexico City. The area produces 200,000 tons a year of prickly pear cactus—up to 10 tons of which ends up as waste on the floor of the cactus market each day. Then, a local green energy startup company got the idea to turn that waste into energy. The generator is now in place at the cactus market, where the vendors are enthusiastic about this new way to utilize the tons of cactus husks that once went directly into the trash.

第 4 段The prickly pear has a number of advantages over other biofuel crops, such as wheat, sugarcane, and soybeans. For one thing, only the husk of the cactus is used for generating biofuel, while its inner flesh is still preserved for food. Therefore, using the plant as a fuel source will not put pressure on food prices. Moreover, the cactus requires minimum water, and thus is not grown on traditional agricultural pasture. This means that no increased competition arises for the water or land presently used for food production.

第 5 段The project in Mexico City has brought new hope to reducing the use of fossil fuels. If it can be expanded, the prickly pear could be the key to Mexico’s energy future.

逐段中譯

第 1 段仙人掌在墨西哥是極具分量的象徵，甚至占據墨西哥國旗上的顯要位置。古代阿茲特克人把仙人掌視為神聖的植物，現代的墨西哥人會吃仙人掌、喝仙人掌汁，甚至用在藥品和洗髮精裡。如今科學家為這種鮮綠色的植物找到了新用途：生產再生能源。

第 2 段這種仙人掌在當地叫做 nopal，墨西哥大規模種植。多年來，人們只用仙人掌裡面可以吃的柔軟嫩肉，布滿尖刺的厚外皮一直視為廢棄物，直到研究人員開發出一種沼氣發電機，把外皮變成電力。

第 3 段開發仙人掌沼氣發電機的試驗計畫，從墨西哥城南部開始。這個地區每年生產 20 萬噸仙人掌，其中每天多達 10 噸成了丟在仙人掌市場地上的廢棄物。後來，當地一家綠能新創公司想到把這些廢棄物變成能源。現在發電機已經在仙人掌市場運轉，以前直接丟進垃圾桶的大量仙人掌外皮，現在可以拿來利用，攤販對這個新方法都很熱衷。

第 4 段和小麥、甘蔗、大豆等其他生質燃料作物相比，仙人掌有幾項優勢。首先，生產生質燃料只用仙人掌的外皮，裡面的嫩肉仍然留下來當食物，所以拿仙人掌當燃料來源，不會對糧食價格造成壓力。此外，仙人掌需要的水極少，不會種在傳統的農地和牧場上，也就是說，目前用來生產糧食的水和土地，不會因此多了競爭。

第 5 段墨西哥城的這項計畫，為減少使用化石燃料帶來了新希望。如果能夠擴大推行，仙人掌可能就是墨西哥能源未來的關鍵。
