The Historical Evolution and Systems of Exchange Rates
指考 109年補考第 48–51 題4 題

文章

第 1 段In finance, an exchange rate is the rate at which one country’s currency is exchanged for the currency of another. The rate is set according to the respective values of the currencies to be exchanged. Before World War I, most currencies were evaluated by the Gold Standard. That is, paper currency issued by a government represented a real amount of gold held by that government. For example, in the 1930s, the British government owned about 8 times as much gold as the U.S. government. Therefore, 1 ounce of gold was worth 4.24 GBP (United Kingdom pound sterling) or 35 USD (U.S. dollars). The difference in the price of gold became the exchange rate for the two currencies: 1GBP was worth 8.25 USD.

第 2 段The Gold Standard started to break down during the Second World War, when European powers printed more money than they had in gold reserves in order to fund military projects. After World War II, the Bretton Woods System was established. The U.S. dollar was chosen as the international reserve currency for trading. Every country knew how much gold a USD was worth, and thus they based the value of their currencies on the USD. All countries were expected to maintain a fixed exchange rate, but were permitted to change it in extraordinary times, such as a recession or inflation.

第 3 段Unfortunately, this system could not keep pace with the fast-changing global economy. As the U.S. increased its military spending, foreign aid, and international investment in the 1960s, it no longer held gold reserves necessary to cover the volume of USD circulation around the world. Other major currencies thus became more valuable and stable compared to the USD, and the Bretton Woods System was finally abolished in 1971.

第 4 段In 1976, the Jamaica Agreement formalized the floating exchange rate system that continues to this day. The value of a country’s currency may vary according to the supply and demand of the foreign exchange market. Countries around the world can also allow their central banks to determine their own exchange rate.

逐段中譯

第 1 段在金融上，匯率是一國貨幣兌換另一國貨幣的比率。匯率依照要兌換的兩種貨幣各自的價值來決定。第一次世界大戰以前，大多數貨幣的價值都以金本位評定。也就是說，政府發行的紙幣，代表政府實際持有的一定數量的黃金。例如在 1930 年代，英國政府擁有的黃金大約是美國政府的 8 倍。因此，1 盎司黃金值 4.24 英鎊（GBP），或 35 美元（USD）。黃金價格的差異，就成了這兩種貨幣的匯率：1 英鎊值 8.25 美元。

第 2 段第二次世界大戰期間，歐洲列強為了支應軍事計畫，印出超過黃金儲備的鈔票，金本位開始瓦解。二戰以後，布列敦森林體系建立。美元獲選為國際貿易的儲備貨幣。每個國家都知道一美元值多少黃金，因此各國都以美元作為訂定本國貨幣價值的基準。所有國家原則上都要維持固定匯率，但遇到經濟衰退或通貨膨脹等特殊時期，可以調整匯率。

第 3 段可惜的是，這個體系跟不上快速變化的全球經濟。1960 年代，美國增加軍事支出、對外援助和國際投資，持有的黃金儲備已經不足以支應全世界流通的美元數量。因此，其他主要貨幣和美元相比，變得更有價值、也更穩定，布列敦森林體系終於在 1971 年廢除。

第 4 段1976 年，牙買加協定正式確立了沿用至今的浮動匯率制度。一國貨幣的價值，可能隨外匯市場的供需而變動。世界各國也可以讓中央銀行決定自己的匯率。
