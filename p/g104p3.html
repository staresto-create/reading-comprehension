104 學測閱讀測驗：攝影家布列松

Photographer Henri Cartier-Bresson
學測 104年第 49–52 題4 題

先讀文章、作答下方的題目，再展開最後的「看答案、依據句與逐段中譯」。也可以到互動練習線上作答，交卷後自動批改。

文章

第 1 段Henri Cartier-Bresson (1908–2004) is one of the most original and influential figures in the history of photography. His humane, spontaneous photographs helped establish photojournalism as an art form.

第 2 段Cartier-Bresson’s family was wealthy—his father made a fortune as a textile manufacturer—but Cartier-Bresson later joked that due to his parents’ frugal ways, it often seemed as though his family was poor.

第 3 段Educated in Paris, Cartier-Bresson developed an early love for literature and the arts. As a teenager, Cartier-Bresson rebelled against his parents’ formal ways of education. In his early adulthood, he even drifted toward communism. But it was art that remained at the center of his life.

第 4 段Cartier-Bresson traveled to Africa in 1931 to hunt antelope and boar. And Africa fueled another interest in him: photography. He then wandered around the world with his camera, using a handheld camera to catch images from fleeting moments of everyday life.

第 5 段Not long after World War II, Cartier-Bresson traveled east, spending considerable time in India, where he met and photographed Gandhi shortly before his assassination in 1948. Cartier-Bresson’s subsequent work to document Gandhi’s death and its immediate impact on the country became one of Life Magazine’s most prized photo essays.

第 6 段Cartier-Bresson’s approach to photography remained much the same throughout his life. He made clear his dislike of images that had been improved by artificial light, darkroom effects, and even cropping. The naturalist in Cartier-Bresson believed that all editing should be done when the photo is taken. In 1952, his first book, The Decisive Moment, a rich collection of his work spanning two decades, was published. “There is nothing in this world that does not have a decisive moment,” he said.

第 7 段In 1968, he began to turn away from photography and returned to his passion for drawing and painting.

題目

49.Which of the following best describes Cartier-Bresson’s family background?

  1. (A)His family was rich but was very economical.
  2. (B)His father went to Paris to open a textile factory.
  3. (C)His wealthy family went bankrupt and became poor.
  4. (D)His parents were very liberal in their ways of education.

50.Which of the following is true about Cartier-Bresson’s career in photography?

  1. (A)He devoted himself to photography all his life.
  2. (B)He developed a passion for photography when he traveled to Africa.
  3. (C)He quit photography right after the publication of The Decisive Moment.
  4. (D)During World War II, he documented the everyday life of the Indian people.

51.What significance did Cartier-Bresson have to Gandhi of India?

  1. (A)He witnessed Gandhi’s assassination in 1948.
  2. (B)He was the first photographer to take Gandhi’s photo.
  3. (C)He used photos to document the effect of Gandhi’s death on India.
  4. (D)His photos told the world who was guilty of assassinating Gandhi.

52.Which of the following is true about Cartier-Bresson’s approach to photography?

  1. (A)He never waited for a decisive moment to shoot photos.
  2. (B)He preferred to edit his images carefully in his darkroom.
  3. (C)Most of his photos described things that happen every day.
  4. (D)He experimented with different ways and settled on being a naturalist.

第 2 段Cartier-Bresson’s family was wealthy—his father made a fortune as a textile manufacturer—but Cartier-Bresson later joked that due to his parents’ frugal ways, it often seemed as though his family was poor.

原文 → 正確選項
wealthy → rich
frugal → economical
第 2 段說布列松家境富裕（wealthy），但父母很節儉（frugal），選項換成 rich 和 economical。(B) 照抄 textile，原文只說父親經營紡織業發了財，去巴黎的是在巴黎受教育的布列松，不是父親去巴黎開工廠；(C) 照抄 poor，原文說的是父母節儉，家裡「看起來好像」很窮，不是真的破產；(D) 照抄第 3 段的 education，第 3 段說他反抗父母拘謹的教育方式，和選項說的開明正好相反。

第 4 段And Africa fueled another interest in him: photography.

原文 → 正確選項

第 4 段說布列松 1931 年到非洲打獵，非洲引發了他另一個興趣：攝影（fueled another interest in him），選項換成 developed a passion for photography。(C) 照抄書名 The Decisive Moment，這本書 1952 年出版，他到 1968 年才開始遠離攝影，不是一出書就放棄；(D) 照抄第 4 段的 everyday life，捕捉日常生活是他周遊世界時的作品，他去印度是在二戰結束後，拍的是甘地；(A) 第 7 段說他晚年轉向素描和繪畫，並沒有一輩子都投入攝影。
第 51 題 細節題 Supporting details

第 5 段Cartier-Bresson’s subsequent work to document Gandhi’s death and its immediate impact on the country became one of Life Magazine’s most prized photo essays.

原文 → 正確選項
impact → effect
the country → India
第 5 段說布列松記錄了甘地之死，以及甘地之死對這個國家立即造成的衝擊（impact on the country），選項把 impact 換成 effect，把 the country 換成 India。(A) 照抄 assassination in 1948，原文說他在甘地遇刺前不久替甘地拍照，沒說他親眼目睹甘地遇刺；(B) 文章沒說他是第一個替甘地拍照的人；(D) 文章沒有談兇手是誰。
第 52 題 細節題（何者為是） Supporting details

第 4 段using a handheld camera to catch images from fleeting moments of everyday life.

原文 → 正確選項
everyday life → happen every day
第 4 段說布列松用手持相機捕捉日常生活（everyday life）中稍縱即逝的瞬間，選項換成 things that happen every day。另外三個選項都照抄第 6 段：(B) 照抄 darkroom，原文說他不喜歡用暗房效果修飾過的照片，正好相反；(D) 照抄 naturalist，原文說他一生拍照的方式幾乎沒變，不是試過各種方法才決定當自然主義者；(A) 照抄 decisive moment，他說世上每件事都有決定性的瞬間，選項卻說他從不等待決定性的瞬間，和他的主張相反。

逐段中譯

第 1 段布列松（Henri Cartier-Bresson，1908–2004）是攝影史上最有原創性、最具影響力的人物之一。他的照片充滿人情味、自然不做作，讓新聞攝影確立為一種藝術形式。

第 2 段布列松家境富裕，父親經營紡織業發了大財；不過布列松後來開玩笑說，因為父母很節儉，家裡常常看起來好像很窮。

第 3 段布列松在巴黎受教育，很早就熱愛文學和藝術。青少年時期，他反抗父母那套拘謹的教育方式。成年初期，他甚至一度傾向共產主義。不過，藝術始終是他生活的重心。

第 4 段1931 年，布列松到非洲獵捕羚羊和野豬。非洲也引發了他另一個興趣：攝影。之後他帶著相機周遊世界，用手持相機捕捉日常生活中稍縱即逝的瞬間。

第 5 段第二次世界大戰結束後不久，布列松往東方旅行，在印度待了相當長的時間。他在印度見到甘地，並替甘地拍照，不久之後甘地就在 1948 年遇刺身亡。布列松後來記錄甘地之死，以及甘地之死對印度立即造成的衝擊，這組作品成為《生活》雜誌最珍貴的攝影專題之一。

第 6 段布列松一生拍照的方式幾乎沒有改變。他明白表示，不喜歡用人工光源、暗房效果，甚至裁切修飾過的照片。布列松是個自然主義者，認為所有的編修都應該在拍攝的當下完成。1952 年，他出版第一本書《決定性的瞬間》，收錄了他二十年來的作品。他說：「這世界上沒有一件事沒有決定性的瞬間。」

第 7 段1968 年，布列松開始逐漸遠離攝影，回到他熱愛的素描和繪畫。
