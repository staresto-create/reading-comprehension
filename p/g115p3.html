115 學測閱讀測驗：地圖方位的歷史演變

The Historical Evolution of Map Orientation
學測 115年第 43–46 題4 題

文章

第 1 段Have you ever wondered why north comes at the top of a map? Well, north may seem a natural choice for the top spot today, but that wasn’t always the case.

第 2 段Documents from ancient times indicate that many maps in early ages were pointing to the east, where the sun rose. In ancient India, for example, maps were most likely oriented to the east. Though there is no physical evidence to support this, the word dakshina “south” in Indian languages also means “right,” suggesting that ancient Indians were oriented toward the east, and therefore south was on their right-hand side. Ample evidences from the Old Testament also suggest that east was at the top of maps in pre-Biblical and Biblical eras, a reason why east is still referred to as the “Orient” today.

第 3 段In the oldest surviving maps, south is at the top, and north points down. Early Egyptian maps showed south on top, most likely because the Nile, vital to Egyptian livelihood, originated in the south. As rivers were believed to flow downward, “up” was therefore south. Map makers in Arabia also drew maps with south on top since the earliest Muslims lived north of Mecca, and a south-oriented map would show the followers looking up toward their holy city.

第 4 段The preference for north arose during the European age of exploration. At the time, sailors relied on the North Star to find their way across the Mediterranean and later the Atlantic. By the 16th century, when Europe’s search for trading routes was at its peak, maps became Eurocentric, with north on top. The expansion of European imperialism in the following centuries further established the “north up” practice as the standard.

第 5 段Today, map orientation is taking on a new perspective. In perhaps our most common interaction with maps—the use of GPS systems on our phones and in our cars, directions have ceased to be as important. The layouts are dynamic, oriented toward our travel path.

第 6 段So, perhaps the north-on-top practice is less a rule and more a blip. After centuries of technological advancements, it seems we’ve ended up right where we began in ancient times: with ourselves in the middle, and our destinations at the top.

逐段中譯

第 1 段你有沒有想過，為什麼地圖的上方是北方？如今北方朝上看起來理所當然，但其實並不是一向如此。

第 2 段古代文獻顯示，早期許多地圖都朝向東方，也就是太陽升起的方向。以古印度為例，當時的地圖很可能以東方為上方。雖然沒有實物證據可以證明這一點，不過印度語言裡表示「南」的 dakshina 這個字也有「右」的意思，可見古印度人面向東方，南方因此在他們的右手邊。舊約聖經裡也有大量證據顯示，在聖經成書之前和聖經時代，地圖上方是東方，這也是東方至今仍叫做 Orient 的原因之一。

第 3 段現存最古老的地圖是南方朝上、北方朝下。早期埃及地圖把南方畫在上面，很可能是因為對埃及人生計至關重要的尼羅河發源於南方；古人認為河水往下流，所以「上」就是南方。阿拉伯的製圖者也把南方畫在上面，因為最早的穆斯林住在麥加北邊，南方朝上的地圖可以讓信徒抬頭望向他們的聖城。

第 4 段北方朝上的習慣，是在歐洲的探險時代形成的。當時水手靠北極星辨認方向，航行地中海，後來又橫渡大西洋。到了十六世紀，歐洲尋找貿易航線的熱潮達到高峰，地圖變成以歐洲為中心、北方朝上。之後幾個世紀歐洲帝國主義擴張，更讓「北方朝上」成為標準做法。

第 5 段如今地圖的方位又有了新的樣貌。我們最常接觸地圖的方式，大概就是用手機和車上的 GPS 系統；在這種情況下，東南西北已經不再那麼重要，畫面會隨時變動，跟著我們的行進路線轉向。

第 6 段所以，北方朝上這個慣例與其說是規則，不如說只是一時的現象。經過幾百年的科技進步，我們似乎又回到了古代的起點：自己在中間，目的地在上方。
