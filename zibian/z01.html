文章

第 1 段For the remote villages of Pakpak Bharat, a district on the Indonesian island of Sumatra, the Lagan–Pagindar road is a lifeline, carrying people to schools, clinics, and government offices. When the road was upgraded in 2024, however, the gap in the forest canopy, the roof of branches high above the ground, grew wider, and the natural pathways that tree-dwelling animals had used for generations disappeared. For critically endangered Sumatran orangutans, which spend nearly their whole lives in the treetops, the new road became a wall. Some 350 of them were split between two patches of forest, unable to reach each other without climbing down to the ground, where traffic and people pose serious dangers.

第 2 段Conservationists decided to rebuild what the road had taken away. Working with local authorities, two organizations, Tangguh Hutan Khatulistiwa and the Sumatran Orangutan Society, put up five rope bridges between the trees. Before choosing the sites, the team studied where the apes nested and how they moved through the forest, and every bridge was designed to carry an orangutan’s weight. Cameras were installed to record who used them. At first, the results were disappointing. Squirrels, langur monkeys, and macaques crossed with ease, and gibbons soon followed, but the orangutans stayed away.

第 3 段Then, after two years, a young male orangutan appeared in front of a camera. He climbed onto a bridge, paused halfway, and made his way to the other side. Conservationists describe it as the first recorded crossing of a public road by a Sumatran orangutan. Earlier bridges for the species had been built mostly over rivers or private logging roads, where there is far less traffic and noise. The director of one of the groups says that orangutans are careful travelers, watching and testing before they commit to a move. The moment matters because isolated groups tend to inbreed, mating with close relatives, which weakens their genes and puts the whole population at risk of collapse. One crossing does not solve that problem, but it suggests that, given time, a simple bridge can help reconnect a divided forest.

每一題的詳解

第 1 題（主旨）答案 (B)

第 1 段說道路把森林隔成兩半，第 2 段寫搭橋，第 3 段寫第一隻猩猩過橋，三段合起來就是 (B)。(A) 說法太過武斷：文章只說隔離的族群有崩潰的風險，沒說會很快滅絕，全篇重點也是補救，不是警告。(C) 以偏概全：第 1 段確實說這條路是村莊的生命線，可是對猩猩而言，這條路造成的是隔離，不是好處。(D) 無中生有：猩猩一直住在被切成兩塊的森林裡，文章沒有寫牠們離開後回來。

第 2 題（細節）答案 (C)

依據在第 1 段 the gap in the forest canopy … grew wider, and the natural pathways … disappeared。答案把 gap 和 pathways 換成「樹冠上的開口變大、原本的路線消失」。(A) 無中生有：文章沒有說道路毀掉了食物來源的樹。(B) 無中生有：noise 只出現在第 3 段，用來比較以前架橋的地點，沒有說噪音把猩猩嚇走。(D) 張冠李戴：道路切斷的是猩猩的通行，村民靠這條路去學校和診所，是第 1 段第一句的內容。

第 3 題（寫作目的）答案 (A)

第 2 段先寫松鼠、葉猴、獼猴輕鬆過橋，長臂猿接著也過了，然後用 but 轉折，說猩猩卻不來。這個安排是要說明橋對部分動物已經有效，猩猩卻還沒有，所以是 (A)。(B) 無中生有：文章沒有說猩猩害怕，第 3 段只說猩猩行動謹慎。(C) 無中生有：文章沒有比較哪一種動物更需要保護。(D) 說法太過武斷：第 2 段說每座橋都設計成能承受猩猩的體重，兩年後第一隻猩猩也過了橋，其他動物先用橋，只代表猩猩比較晚才用。

第 4 題（與文章相符的敘述）答案 (D)

依據在第 2 段 the team studied where the apes nested and how they moved through the forest。答案把「研究牠們在哪裡築巢、怎麼移動」換成「了解猩猩的習性」。(A) 張冠李戴：架在河上方的是第 3 段提到的「以前的橋」，這五座橋是架在公路上方的樹與樹之間。(B) 張冠李戴：第 3 段寫的是 after two years，也就是橋架好兩年後才出現第一隻猩猩。(C) 說法太過武斷：第 3 段明說 One crossing does not solve that problem，一次過橋不代表近親繁殖的問題已經解決。
